# Research Design

This study employs a sequential explanatory mixed-methods research design to comprehensively evaluate the development, deployment, and institutional impact of SWAFROTECH, an AI-driven compliance and violation management system at De La Salle University-Dasmariñas (DLSU-D). The quantitative phase focuses on the rigorous technical validation of the system's core algorithms. For Module 1, the YOLO11-based dress code detection model is evaluated using standardized computer vision metrics, specifically Mean Average Precision (mAP@0.5), precision, recall, and inference speed, across a balanced dataset of 119,282 images to ensure highly reliable, bias-free garment classification. Concurrently, in Module 2, quantitative analysis is applied to measure the computational efficiency of the Haversine distance-based Greedy Nearest Neighbor (GNN) patrol optimization algorithm and the mathematical accuracy of the Exponential Temporal Decay risk scoring mechanism ($\lambda=0.023$) in dynamically adjusting student behavioral risk indices. 

Following the technical validation, the qualitative phase investigates the system's practical usability and impact on campus discipline. Through structured surveys, usability testing (such as the System Usability Scale), and key informant interviews, empirical feedback is gathered from all key stakeholders. This includes the Student Welfare and Formation Office (SWAFO) officers who utilize the live AI monitor and mobile patrol routing, the system administrators responsible for managing dynamic handbook policies, and the DLSU-D students interacting with their personal compliance portals and the Gemini-powered AI handbook chatbot. By synthesizing quantitative algorithmic performance data with qualitative user-experience feedback, this mixed-methods approach provides a holistic assessment of SWAFROTECH’s effectiveness in automating policy enforcement, ensuring procedural fairness, and modernizing the disciplinary ecosystem at DLSU-D.
